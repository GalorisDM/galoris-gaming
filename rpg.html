The big picture

From the Elder Ones to the Third Age

History is reduced to the events players are most likely to need, while keeping the setting’s names and cause-and-effect intact.

5Elder Ones
3Major ages
617Years since the Second Age ended
9thCurrent mortal spell ceiling
Creation: Titia (life), Betorr (death), Xabrathon (chaos), Aznir (order), and Jiarus (time) formed Galoris together. Titia created life and gifted magic; Aznir shaped order; Xabrathon formed mountains and upheaval; Betorr made the underworld; Jiarus bound all of it within time.

The First Age — creation and the Elder War

Xabrathon grew resentful of an increasingly ordered world, destroyed Betorr, seized death, and prevented proper dying—giving rise to the first undead. War followed. Xabrathon’s forces included fiends, corrupted peoples, and chromatic dragons; the opposing Elder Ones were supported by mortal heroes and metallic dragons.

Xabrathon is sealed; heroes ascend

Xabrathon was defeated and imprisoned in the ocean depths. The remaining Elder Ones withdrew from Galoris. Great mortal heroes from both sides were elevated into the first gods so the world would still be watched without the Elder Ones directly ruling it.

The Second Age — great kingdoms and unrestricted magic

The elven realm of Tor’danedial rose in western Elant, while the human Empire of Trajorn dominated the east. Dwarven kingdoms flourished in the northern mountains. Anora allowed deep access to the weave, enabling magic later ages could not reproduce.

The long peace fractures

Maltrix, Zonia, Wuella, and Dragaron secretly cultivated darker peoples and conflicts. Drow raids struck elven lands; orcs, ogres, trolls, and other threats pressed into human territories.

617 years ago — the Second Age ends

Doppelgangers serving Maltrix and Wuella infiltrated Trajorn and assassinated Emperor Titas Trajorn IV, igniting civil war. At the same time, the elven wizard Galaxain Tumaril accepted the patronage of Dragaron and Zonia and became the world’s first lich-like Balnorn.

Galaxain’s cataclysm and the War of Light and Dark

Galaxain tore a volcano from the earth, burning vast elven forests and darkening much of the world with ash. Anora restricted the weave so mortal magic could no longer exceed ninth level. The gods returned to patron mortal forces; Galaxain was eventually sealed in the Grip of Ash.

The Third Age — the current world

The gods now openly grant patronage but are expected to balance one another. Ancient populations never fully recovered. Elves live in smaller communities, human kingdoms dominate much of the south and east, and ruins of older civilizations lie throughout the wilds.

Climate & Calendar

Enough detail to establish travel tone, seasons, and datekeeping without making players read a gazetteer.

North

Crest of the World

Far northern Elant is tundra. The mountains just south are cold-temperate, with long biting winters and warm summers.

Middle

Temperate Elant

South of the Kingdom of Stromgar, the climate becomes moderate temperate: cold winters and hot summers.

South

Warmer Lands

Temperatures rise toward the equator, becoming tropical or desert-like only in the far south. Southern summers can be humid or sharply dry depending on the region.

12-month calendar

Galoris uses a 12-month calendar with month lengths matching the familiar 31/30/28-day pattern and a leap year every four years. Each formal month also has a common name used in speech as “the Month of …”. The packet excerpt provides the structure but not the month-name list.

Kingdom of Valacia

Playable locations at a glance

High-value facts are surfaced first; social texture and adventure hooks stay one click away.

Village

Brookhollow

A small farming community in northeastern Valacia, set in foothills near northern mountains, thick forest, and ancient ruins that still draw adventurers.

Population716
LeadershipReeve Salwyn “Sal” Marsh + council of elders
Defense20–30 rotating militia on duty
CharacterFarms, hunters, retired adventurers
Commerce & organizations
Traveling merchants and peddlers bring most exotic goods. Salwyn’s Trading Post is the local commercial hub. A temple to Tenera serves the farming population, with shrines to Bukoni, Aldir, and Anora. The village also has a council of elders and a socially influential “knitting circle.”
The Dancing Lady
A large three-story inn with 12 guest rooms, a busy common room, strong food reputation, and locally famous hard cider. Proprietor Ezmerelda “Zelda” is jovial but formidable; her husband Mika is a retired adventurer missing a hand.
River town

Dardin Falls

West of Brookhollow, this tightly packed trade town sits on a major tributary and serves as the smaller village’s commercial “big brother.”

Population2,604
RulerBaron Helleirth Highmantle
DefenseGuard, sworn knights, and 200–250 muster potential
EconomyRiver trade and dwarven goods
Commerce & faith
Trade flows from local lands and the dwarven kingdom of Dornhammer through the Dardin Tributary and Gniss River. Zelander has the largest temple; Axnus and Gibeus also have established worship. Major trading interests include the West Independent Company, Trader’s Guild, and Mithril Merchants.
The Bound & The Three Legged Sow
Dardin Falls has an organized criminal presence tied to the Bound in Borahn. The Three Legged Sow near the docks caters to commoners, adventurers, and rougher clientele. Owner Marcus Dwillin is believed to have an understanding with the Bound and employs five half-orc bouncers.
Capital of Valacia

Borahn district guide

Use the district cards as a session-prep index: tone, function, danger, and a memorable hook.

The Rookery of Tor Valluum
Borahn’s northeastern docks and slums. River traffic from the Gniss, Tazuew, and Dardin systems enters here. The docks are controlled, but the damp maze beyond is poor, crowded, and strongly influenced by the Bound. The Broken Candle is a notable low-cost inn.
The Lilac Commons
The wealthy northeastern district of noble towers, palaces, and powerful merchant households, named for the pale lilac stone used in its architecture. It is heavily guarded and shadowed by rumors of illicit fight clubs and illegal flesh trade.
Crystal Gardens of Goethe
A vast botanical garden and wildlife reserve built around towering colored crystals. Magic keeps visitors on safe paths and stabilizes the climate, while curators use special crystals to cross the barriers.
Tiered Palace of Valacia
The royal palace crowns the city’s highest hill. Four fortified baileys climb toward a central castle, and the entire hill functions like a town-sized defensive complex. Rumors persist of extensive tunnels and caverns below.
The Library Eternal
Borahn’s scholar district, centered on a magically expanded repository run by the Scrivners. Anora is revered here. The mysterious Tower of Concordance sometimes vanishes and later returns to the same site.
The Mercantilin
The main merchant district and home to Borahn’s grand bazaar. Nearly every imaginable trade good can be found here, along with arcane materials, hawkers, beggars, and pickpockets. The Bound are rumored to organize some street activity.
The Weaver’s Cross
A well-kept middle-class district south of the palace. Neighborhoods organize much of their own upkeep, and prosperous families often grow here before eventually moving into the Lilac Commons.
The Temple District
A dense concentration of temples and shrines. Aldir, Anora, Axnus, and Gibeus have the largest temples, while Zelander, Bukoni, Tenera, and Etdar also have established worship. Hidden shrines to darker deities are rumored underground.
Radiant Spires of Time and Infinity
Two sealed towers in the southwest, linked by visible arcane energy. They have resisted force, spells, and guile for roughly four centuries. Local prophecy says they may open in Valacia’s darkest hour.
The Silkmore
A heavily guarded industrial district producing one of Borahn’s major exports: silk. Recent rumors claim attempts to breed larger spiders have led to dog-sized specimens on city rooftops.
The Artist Quarters
A district of theaters, galleries, performers, and ambitious architecture. Patronage from nobles and merchants sustains much of the artistic life, though that patronage can come with social pressure.
The Fogwalk Expanse
The southern docks, laced with canals and bridges. Wealthier shipping interests dominate here, controls are tighter than in the Rookery, and evening fog gives the district its name. A strict curfew creates opportunities for risk-takers.
